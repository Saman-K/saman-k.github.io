On a full-suspension bike the shock often blocks the usual spot under the top tube. Any other solid part of the frame works if it's high enough and near the centre of mass, front to back. The top of the rear triangle, under the seat stays, is a common choice. Never rest the stand on the shock.

How to measure your bike

1. Find the contact point. This is where the stand will touch your bike. It can be any part of the bike that is:

  • solid: part of the frame, not something that moves or bends, such as a cable, a shock or a bottle cage;
  • high enough to give a stable lean; and
  • close to the bike's centre of mass, front to back. Too far forward or back and the bike tips over the front or rear wheel.

The usual choice is the underside of the top tube, close to where it meets the seat tube. On a full-suspension bike, the top of the rear triangle under the seat stays often works. Measure the one you plan to use.

2. Stand the bike upright. It must be vertical, not leaning. Get someone to hold it, or rest it against a wall with both wheels square to the floor. Don't lean the bike and guess where the stand will meet the ground.

3. Measure straight down. Measure from the contact point straight down to the ground, in millimetres. The chain stays might get in your way, so take your time. Measure twice, cut once.

4. Type it in. Enter it as the contact height. The calculator adds a little length for the stand's angle, then works out the length of every section.

Being a few millimetres out won't matter. The stand still works.
